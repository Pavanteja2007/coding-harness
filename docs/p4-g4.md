## 0. What "pass" means here, and what it does not

The gate asks whether a 3+ session task completes correctly **and is measured**.
Both halves are true, and the second half required making three of the four
arms real before it could be true at all.

**What G4 does NOT claim:**

- Not that the initializer helps. The three outcome metrics
  (`completion`, `rework_rate`, `constraint_retention`) **do not separate the
  arms**, and `constraint_retention` is a **null** in the `baseline` arm by
  construction.
- Not that compaction is insufficient. Nothing here touches compaction; P3a
  measured that.
- Not that a real model benefits. **Every model call is a scripted double.**
  `MODEL_QUALITY_MEASURED = False` is on every receipt.
- Not that the arms are equally informative. Two of four separate;
  `subagent_isolation` is an **exercised-but-unstressed null** (§1.4).

## 1. The multi-session result

### 1.1 The arms were NOT real until this wave, and that was the blocking finding

P4/W1 shipped a four-arm evaluation whose `read_before_edit` and
`subagent_isolation` arms were **structurally incapable of separating**: its
session body (`_scripted_session`) wrote each feature's implementation directly
with `path.write_text`, so neither mechanism key was read by anything on the
path a session actually took. Those two arms were void, and
**`Never run an ablation where one arm is void`** forbids reporting them.

Three defects fixed before a number was produced:

| # | defect | consequence had it shipped |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `_note_session` created `PROGRESS.md` unconditionally | the `baseline` arm — which has **no initializer** — was handed a progress ledger, which then counted as a changed file in `rework_rate` and inflated the baseline's parent context. **The instrument was changing what it measured.** |
| 2 | `ToolRegistry.read_ledger` starts as `None` and is only created by `begin_turn` | the read-before-edit gate returned `None` immediately ("no ledger, no gate") while the registry's own receipt reported `armed_and_did_nothing: False` — **a silent no-op gate reported as armed.** The arm was invisible for a second, independent reason. |
| 3 | the control ran one session over **one** feature, then asked whether the *other* features still failed | demonstrated nothing: of course they were, the control had never tried them. |

The session body is now `_bounded_session`: every turn is a real
`ToolRegistry.execute` through the product's guard chain, `session_turns` is
**enforced**, verification is explicitly *not* a turn (a verifier is not a model
turn), and `subagent_isolation` moves the work into a bounded subagent with its
own message list.

`evals.multi_session.arm_realness()` publishes three independent verdicts per arm
and they are computed, not claimed:

| `read_before_edit` | **`initializer`** | `read_before_edit` | yes | no |

### 1.5 Variance — published, not implied

`spread()` reports `n`, `min`, `median`, `max` and `distinct` per metric per arm.

**Every measured metric had `distinct == 1` over three repetitions: the run has
ZERO variance.** The boundary is deterministic, so three repetitions are a
*non-determinism check*, not an error bar. Reporting "median of 3" without that
would dress a deterministic number as a statistical one.

**That sentence was wrong when first written, and it was wrong because the
receipt was broken.** `spread()` read its values off the repetition records,
which carry their numbers under `per_task_metrics` rather than at the top level,
so every lookup was `None` and every arm published `n: 0, values: [],
no_variance: True`. An empty list has `distinct == 0`, so `max(..., default=0.0)`
collapsed the noise floor to **zero measured from nothing** — and
`exceeds_own_noise` came out `True` for every metric that moved.

The conclusion survived and the receipt did not, which is the uncomfortable
shape: read from `Metric.per_session` (the same per-repetition aggregates the
median is computed from), `n: 3` and `distinct: 1` really is zero variance.
**A right answer reached through a broken instrument is not yet a measurement**,
and the difference is invisible unless somebody reads `n`.

`baseline/constraint_retention` and every `cost_usd` row now report `n: 0` and
`no_variance: None` — **not** `True` — because zero observations is the absence
of evidence rather than evidence of stability. `exceeds_own_noise` is `False`
wherever `noise_measured` is `False`: a gap cannot exceed a floor nobody
measured. Two pins hold both halves.

`discrimination()` answers the honest reader's question — `exceeds_own_noise`:
is the gap between two arms larger than the widest run-to-run spread inside one
arm? With a **measured** zero within-arm spread, every observed gap does.
**The reader must not mistake that for a confidence interval: it is a statement
that the noise floor is zero, not that the effect is large.**

Separating: `parent_context_tokens`, `rework_rate`, `tokens_per_session`.

### 1.6 The honest verdict, including the part that is null

1. **Completion is 1.0 in every arm.** The initializer does not change whether a
   4-session effort finishes. On this corpus the scripted session is competent
   enough that finishing is not the binding constraint.
2. **`rework_rate` moves — and moves AGAINST the initializer (0.0 → 0.5).**
   This is the most substantive finding in the measurement, and it is *not* an
   artefact. One feature per session means feature files never collide, so the
   **only** file a later session rewrites is `PROGRESS.md` — the ledger the
   initializer creates. **The ledger discipline introduces rework on the ledger
   itself.** A session must read, extend and rewrite what every earlier session
   wrote. That is the price of the discipline, measured, in the only direction
   the metric points on this corpus.
3. **The read-before-edit gate costs 2 turns and ~7% more tokens per session**
   (1 → 3 turns, 992.5 → 1062.0 heuristic tokens). At a 3-turn budget it still
   completes, so the cost is **turns, not completion**. This is why
   `control_arm_turn_arithmetic()` publishes that the budget accommodates the
   gate exactly: a gate engineered into a completion failure would have been
   reporting a budget problem as a mechanism result.
4. **`subagent_isolation` is an EXERCISED-BUT-NOT-STRESSED NULL.** It ran; its
   receipt is published on every session; and it compressed **nothing** — the
   largest child transcript was **25 characters against an 8 000-character cap
   (0.31% of the limit)**. The mechanism was never stressed. **This is a
   property of a four-module synthetic shop, NOT evidence that isolation fails
   to save context**, and the report says so in those words rather than letting
   the arm's identical numbers read as a result.
5. **Nothing decays by session 4.** The brief's most-expected finding — the
   initializer helping early and decaying — **did not occur, and could not have
   been observed**: the scripted session writes one correct implementation per
   turn, so there is no session-4 behaviour capable of decaying. `constraint_retention`
   is 1.0 in session 4 of every arm that has constraints. **The decay question
   is UNANSWERED, not answered "no".**

### 1.7 Two tasks, and the shapes still uncovered

Pin #5 (one task only) is **CLOSED**. The set is now:

- `multi_session_shop` — four **independent** features. Answers *does a durable
  brief survive to a later session at all*.
- `multi_session_cascade` — four features in a **dependency chain** (feature N
  imports feature N-1). Answers *does the ledger stop a later session redoing
  earlier work*, which the shop shape structurally cannot reach.

Asserted on the **generated source**, not a shape string, so the two cannot
differ only in module names.

`TASK_SHAPES_NOT_COVERED` names four shapes still missing, published on every
report: constraints recoverable **only** from the progress ledger; a later session
having to revert an earlier one; sessions interleaving on one file; and a
real-model task where a session might decline its work. **The most important
missing one is the first**: every session in both tasks receives the same
`CONSTRAINTS` tuple from the brief, so constraint retention is currently measured
against a brief that always carried them.

## 3. The e2e verification capability, and what it does not cover

T2's `execution/e2e_verify.py` gives an `e2e` feature a real three-phase
(`setup` / `drive` / `teardown`) run, and **teardown runs even when a drive step
fails** — a dev server left running makes the next session's result
unreproducible.

**What it does not cover, measured:** `EndToEndReport` carries exactly
`available, duration_s, feature_id, outcome, ran, steps, teardown_error,
teardown_ran, unavailable_reason` — **nine fields, no regression term and no
flake term.** An `e2e` feature is marked `passing` on strictly weaker evidence
than a `command` feature, which reaches the repository's repetitions and
three-valued flake verdict. Safe direction, real capability gap. **Recorded, and
T5's own assessment is that it is NOT going to close in P4** — it needs a
decision about what an end-to-end regression *means*.

### 4.1 The integrity audit — and the one real gap it found

**Six tamper paths, re-run against the REAL store: 6/6 HANDLED.** Five are
detected after the fact by reading disk; one (`apply_status` with no evidence)
is refused at write, which is the stronger outcome and is scored as such.

Then the **drift** audit, which the six cannot see — *many individually-valid
writes, cumulatively a self-graded report card*:

| probe | verdict |
|---|---|
| **CONTROL** — N legitimate evidence-backed passes | `CLEAN` (coverage 1.0) |
| `replace_document(reseal=True)` narrowing a feature's check from `pytest -k receipts` to `pytest --collect-only` | **`HANDLED`** |
| session 4 marking a feature passing on session 1's evidence digest | **`HANDLED` — was NOT** |
| `passing → failing → passing` across eight sessions | `DOCUMENTED_IN_HISTORY` |

**The re-seal probe is the most valuable result in this section, and it is a
positive one.** `replace_document(document, reseal=True)` is a *sanctioned*
authoring API that re-signs the ledger without requiring evidence — the obvious
drift is one call. Measured: the narrowed command **is** written to disk, and the
guard is **RED** afterwards with `item_mutated` + `document_not_in_history`. The
history chain still records the pre-narrowing digest, so a fresh seal over a
changed obligation is a **mismatch**, not a fresh start. **The seal is not a
rubber stamp an authorised caller can re-press.**

**The gap that was real, found by the audit, fixed and pinned.** Before this
wave, `apply_status` recorded the `actor` and checked the evidence resolved, said
`pass`, and came from the feature's own command — **but nothing compared the
evidence's own `session` to the actor.** So session 4 could mark a feature
`passing` by citing session 1's digest. Every check passed. Four such writes
report **four sessions of verified work on the strength of one run.**

Fixed in `memory/feature_ledger_store.py`: new closed slug
`evidence_session_mismatch`, refusal naming **both** sessions, an explicit
`allow_cross_session_evidence=True` override for the legitimate re-affirmation
case, and `evidence_session` + `cross_session_evidence` on every history row so
an override is **enumerable rather than inferable**. Placed **after**
`_require_passing_evidence`, so an unusable digest is still reported as such and
the new check only ever sees evidence that is real.

### 7.3 The finding this gate was most prepared for, and what actually happened

The brief: *"the initializer helps session 1 and session 2, and the benefit decays
by session 4 — because the ledger, like any summary, loses information. If that
is what happens, it is the most valuable finding Phase 4 produces, and it points
directly at Phase 3a's incremental-compaction work."*

**What actually happened: it did not decay, and the question is unanswered.**

`constraint_retention` is **1.0 in session 4** of every arm that has constraints.
But that is not evidence the ledger *retains* information — the constraints are
re-rendered **in full into every session's brief** by `SessionBrief.render()`, so
nothing is being carried across a boundary at all. The survival-vs-compaction
distinction is genuinely under test for **history** (a planted marker in session
1's conversation is absent from session 3's brief) but **not for the ledger's own
content**.

**So the decay question is open, and the measurement that would answer it is
named:** a task whose constraints are recoverable **only** from the progress
ledger — the first entry in `TASK_SHAPES_NOT_COVERED`. Until it exists, any claim
that the initializer's benefit decays is unmeasured in both directions.
